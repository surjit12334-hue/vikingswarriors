Taekwondo History

Taekwondo is a Korean martial art whose name means "the way of the foot and fist". Its roots reach back to older Korean fighting arts such as taekkyeon and subak, developed over centuries on the Korean peninsula, with modern kickboxing and karate techniques folded in during the twentieth century. The name was formalised in 1955 by General Choi Hong-hi, and the two major world bodies followed soon after: the International Taekwon-Do Federation in 1966 and World Taekwondo (then the World Taekwondo Federation) in 1973. After being a demonstration sport at the 1988 and 1992 Olympics, taekwondo became a full medal event at the Sydney 2000 Games, where it has stayed ever since. Today it is practised by tens of millions of people in more than 200 countries, and remains built around the dobok uniform, the belt ranking system, and the five tenets our academy lives by.
